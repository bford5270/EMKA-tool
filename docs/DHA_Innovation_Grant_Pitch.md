## The email

**To:** [Innovation program POC / DHA J-9 / MHS innovation inbox]
**Cc:** [Commanding Officer, 1st Medical Battalion] · [1st MLG Surgeon] · [I MEF Surgeon]
**Subject:** Innovation Grant Request — $10,000: Delivering Knowledge at the Point of Care in DDIL Environments

[Salutation],

I am requesting **$10,000 in [program name] innovation funds** for a 90-day prototype
of the Expeditionary Medical Knowledge Assistant (EMKA), an air-gapped, PHI-free
clinical reference system for deployed use. The case is best made through the
data–information–knowledge–wisdom progression.

**We already own the data.** Joint Trauma System clinical practice guidelines, CoTCCC
guidelines, VA/DoD CPGs, and the NTTP 4-02 series total thousands of pages the
Department has already funded and published. Sitting on a hard drive, they are inert.

**Structure turns data into information.** EMKA ingests those publications into a
citation-indexed corpus — every paragraph tagged with its publication, edition,
authority tier, and page.

**Retrieval turns information into knowledge.** A corpsman asks a plain-language
question and receives, in under 30 seconds and with no network connection, the
passage that answers it: quoted verbatim, machine-verified against the source, and
cited to the page. Not a list of search results to work through — the answer, with
its proof attached.

**Wisdom remains with the clinician.** The system does not decide. It puts the right
knowledge in front of the person who does, inside the window where it still changes
the outcome.

The gap in deployed medicine is not knowledge. It is access to knowledge under
EMCON, bandwidth denial, and MASCAL time pressure. This closes it.
